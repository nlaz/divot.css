The divot — button specimen sheet

Every button here has a face at exactly its ground: same material as the surface behind it, zero height, nothing to fall from. It exists because a groove is cut around it. The walls of that groove are mixed from the ground via color-mix, never hardcoded — which is what lets one recipe survive a surface change, a theme flip and the paper special case without an override. This page links the real css/tokens.css and css/core.css; if it looks wrong, the library is wrong.

This is css/divot.css — the button and nothing else. The build-out (wells, tabs, cards, popovers, menus, forms, and the width, elevation and radius axes) has its own sheet: system.html →

§1 · Strength — one number, 0–100

§2 · States

Tactility by value, not displacement

Nothing translates. Hover deepens the cut; press sinks the face to the floor of the groove, deleting the inner wall — there is no wall climbing back up to a face that has sunk. :active and the latched classes are the same gesture: a toggle that stays on is a button still being held down.

§3 · Roles

Six, including a null one

quiet mixes its walls to exactly the ground rather than removing them, so the border still holds its space and the groove appears on hover without reflowing the row. bare is the shared class with no walls at all — for full-width ledger and rail rows, where a groove around every line would fight the surface.

§4 · The raise

One step down, always

The inverse of the divot at one band instead of two: the face lifts and the chisel flips to highlight-top-left. No drop shadow — a hard shadow is what made the old buttons read as floating. Every button in the system travels exactly one step down on press: a default goes flush → sunk, a raise goes raised → flush, landing on the divot's rest state.

§5 · Grounds

The cut belongs to the surface, not the button

Re-point --divot-ground on the container and the recipe follows. This is the part that breaks if you hardcode the walls: a button cut for --bg and then placed on --well stops being flush and starts being a rectangle.

Paper is the instructive one. Nothing is lighter than a white scan, so --divot-src-hi collapses to the paper itself and the groove becomes shadow-only — which is what a groove in white paper actually looks like. Paper also pins its ink, because a scan is white whatever the hour.

§7 · Both themes

Same CSS, swapped tokens

The theme follows prefers-color-scheme on its own; these panels force the two token sets side by side with [data-theme] so the walls can be compared. The button system has no colours of its own.

§8 · Token API

TokenDefaultWhat it does
--divot-n45Strength, 0–100. The only knob you should normally touch.
--divot-groundvar(--bg)The plane being cut. Must equal what is actually behind the button. Set it on the container, not the button.
--divot-src-hivar(--edge-hi)Light source for the up-slope. Collapse it to the ground where nothing can be lighter (paper).
--divot-src-lovar(--edge-lo)Shadow source for the down-slope.
--raise-n20How far .raise lifts off the plane.
--divot-bump0Internal. Lets :hover deepen the cut without a cyclic reference. Don't set it at call sites.

§9 · Rules

  • Set the ground on the container. Every surface that is not --bg declares its own --divot-ground once, and writes its own background from that same variable so the two cannot drift.
  • Never hardcode the wall colours. The moment a hex goes into a border, the button stops surviving theme flips — and the theme follows prefers-color-scheme, so that failure ships silently to half your sessions.
  • Don't scale the profile. It is a physical cut, not a proportion. Same 2px on a 22px icon toggle and a 44px primary.
  • Ghost and quiet only where context implies a target — inside a toolbar, a group, a row of peers.
  • Inversion is for severity, not emphasis. Re-point --divot-ground to --ink rather than setting a background, so the walls re-derive.
  • Keep the focus ring. A flush button has no value contrast against its ground; the outline is the one element permitted to break the plane.

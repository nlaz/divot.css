divot.css

A simple skeuomorphic button style, for buttons that feel as good to press as the real thing.

DownloadGitHub

why

I got tired of making the same button.

I've been building software for years, and every project ends at the same control: a rectangle, some padding, a border, a fill. Then the same rectangle again in another colour for the next state. It works, and it stopped being interesting a long time ago. This stylesheet started as a way out of that rut.

Real buttons are satisfying to press. There's travel, a face that goes down and comes back, a click you can feel. I wanted a page to have some of that: not a picture of a button, but something with a position. So a divot is cut into the page rather than laid on top of it. The face sits flush with the surface, and a soft groove around it is the only thing that says "press me".

What makes it different from other bevelled buttons is that flush is the resting state, and a button can move one step either way. There's a raised version that stands one band above the surface, and a sunk version, which is where every press lands. Three positions, one material, no accent colour. It gives a page a tangible feel that makes me want to click things, and that's the whole reason for it.

the concept

A groove, not a box.

A divot is a button cut into the page instead of laid on top of it. The face is the same colour as whatever is behind it. A 2px groove around the edge is the only thing that says "button": one wall down, one wall back up, light from the top-left. Both wall colours are mixed from the background with color-mix(), so one class works on every surface and in both themes.

The same class on four surfaces. Nothing is overridden; the walls re-mix from whatever is behind them.

levels

Three positions, one step apart.

Flush is the resting state. Every press travels exactly one step down: a raised button lands flush, a flush button lands sunk.

+1

Raised. Stands one band above the surface. The one button on a screen that should be pressed first: save, send, confirm. There is no accent colour; this is how a button gets emphasis.

.divot.raise
0

Flush. The default. The face is the page. Use it for everything that isn't the primary action, which is most buttons.

.divot
−1

Sunk. Where a press lands, and where a toggle stays while it's on. Filters, switches, a tab that is selected.

.divot[aria-pressed="true"]

reference

Every role.

One class plus a role. None of them change the box; depth is contrast, not size.

.divotThe default. Flush with the page.
heldA toggle that is on. Also .on.
.raiseOne band up. The primary action.
.quietNo walls until hovered. Toolbars.
.ghostLighter cut. Inside a framed group.
.firmHeavier cut. Over busy grounds.
.bareNo walls at all. List and rail rows.
.iconSquares the box around a glyph.
.eased3px corners.
.pillFull-round. Chips and filters.
.dangerInverts on hover. Severity, not emphasis.
:disabledGroove flat, ink faded, box unchanged.

customize

Move the lamp.

8× · press it

Every wall on the page takes its light from one value. Drag the lamp around the ring to move it. The button inside is a real .divot at eight times scale, so you can press it.

:root { --divot-light: 315deg; }

install

Link it.

one file
<link rel="stylesheet" href="divot.css">
<button class="divot">Read</button>
on a surface that isn't the page
<div class="ground-surface">
  <button class="divot">Read</button>
</div>

Needs color-mix(): Safari 16.2, Chrome 111, Firefox 113 and later. Follows the system theme; force one with data-theme="light" or "dark" on any element.

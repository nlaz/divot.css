The divot past the button. This sheet links css/tokens.css and css/core.css, then loads css/system.css over the top — the same three files css/divot.system.css imports for you — so everything below is running against the two derived colours the core primitive draws with, and nothing on this page is a mockup. §1 · The one change to the primitive

The walls stop being a border

An inset box-shadow with an offset and no blur paints an L. Two of them — at w and 2w, near one listed first — give the same down-wall and back-up wall the border gave, and buy two things it could not: a width that never reflows the row, and a radius with no four-colour miter to go wrong. It also makes .quiet honest: there is no longer a border to hold space, so nothing can reflow when the groove appears. At w:1 the two renderers are the same picture — the proposed box is 2px smaller because it has no border, which is a padding change at promotion time and nothing more.

§2 · Depth — --divot-n, 0–100

How far the walls travel, not how big they are

Unchanged from the core's meaning, and still the only knob most call sites should touch. Depth is contrast: a ghost and a firm are the same 2px profile and the same box, and only the wall values move.

§3 · Width — --divot-w, in bands

The new axis, and the one to be careful with

Width is how many pixels of the face the cut eats; depth is how dark it goes. They are genuinely independent — a wide shallow groove is a chamfer, a narrow deep one is a score line — and the matrix below is the argument for keeping them apart. The house rule still stands for buttons: don't scale the profile, a 22px icon toggle and a 44px primary get the same w:1. Width is for the objects a button sits in — cards at w:2, where 1px of edge on a 280px plane reads as a hairline rather than a cut.

n × w — depth down, width across

§4 · Elevation — --divot-rest, −2 … +2

Raised slightly, lowered slightly

One signed number. The face mixes toward the shadow source going down and the light source going up; the outer wall rotates rather than flipping at a threshold, passing through flat mid-grey at ±1 — which is what a 1px lip actually looks like in raking light. The return wall fades out at both extremes, because a face on the floor of its groove has no wall climbing back to it and a face standing proud has no groove.

The stops are not new inventions: −2 is exactly the core's pressed state, 0 is the core's rest state and +2 is within a hair of its .raise. What is new is ±1, and the fact that press is now arithmetic — every button travels two bands down from wherever it was authored, so .raise lands on flush and a default lands on the floor, with one rule instead of four.

§5 · Radius

Rounded, and where the cheap renderer starts to lie

The bands follow the curve because inset shadows are clipped by border-radius. Up to --r-md that is exact. Past it the offset-band renderer only knows four directions, while on a real curve the wall's angle to the light changes continuously — so at pill radius the top band runs flat across an arc that should be shading off, and the flatness is the only thing you see. .pill therefore swaps to a conic gradient, which is the same lighting model told the truth: light from the top-left means the wall is darkest at 315° and lightest at 135°, smoothly.

Whether you should use any of this is a separate question from whether the primitive can survive it. --r stays 0px; these are opt-in per component. The honest case for the pill is the one place a shape already means something — a switch knob, a filter chip, a count.

§6 · Tabs — the file cabinet

Selection is elevation

The ladder is what makes a folder tab possible without a fill. Folders that are not open sit back in the drawer at −1; the open one stands at +1, gets taller rather than moving, and laps one band over the panel so the panel's top wall stops at it. Its face and the panel's face are the same piece of card because they are derived from the same ground. That is the same argument .docitem makes for weight and ink — no fill, no rule, no colour.

Two cuts. Folder rounds the top corners, which is what a manila folder does and what the band renderer draws correctly. Cut takes the trapezoid literally with a clip-path — which kills box-shadow, so its wall is a 1px underlay showing past an inset copy of the same shape, in one averaged colour, because a shoulder running from the lit side to the shadowed side has no single right answer. Use the first; the second is here because the shoulders are the picture people have in their head.

§7 · Cards

Four, and the choice is a claim about what the thing is

Flush is a region of this surface with an edge — the default, and the only one that survives a grid of twenty without the page turning to gravel. Lift is something on the surface, for the one card being acted on. Tray is a hole things go into: drop targets, queues, empty states — the only card that pins its face, to --well, so it matches the fields elsewhere on the page. Pick is a divot at card scale: it presses, it latches, it is a button.

Cards are where --divot-w earns its keep. These are at w:2: 1px of edge around a 280px plane reads as a printed rule, 2px reads as a cut.

§8 · Popovers and menus

The hard dithered drop stays

It is the one thing in the system that says over the page, not in it, and it is two aligned dither rectangles rather than a box-shadow because a blurred copy of the box would be a different material. What the ladder adds is the elevation: a popover sits at +1, so its own edge is a lip instead of a rule, and an arrow carries the same two walls round to point at its anchor. Menu rows are .bare per the house rules — a groove around every line fights the surface — and the hovered row is the one that gets cut, which doubles as the only cue for where the keyboard is.

§9 · Fields — the well

The same recipe at −2, pinned

The house rules already state the pair: a groove around a face is a button, a well you can see into is a field, and the two must never share a treatment. Under one profile that distinction has to be carried by something, and it is carried by two facts — a field is cut deeper (--well-n: 55, because it has to out-read a button beside it in the same toolbar) and a field never travels. Focus deepens the cut; it does not move the face. A pressed button is momentary, a field is permanent.

Checks, radios, switches and ranges are all the same idea: a well holding something. Nothing is filled with colour to mean on — the accent is a highlighter, not a state, and greyscale would lose it. On is ink in a hole, or a knob that has moved.

§10 · Forms

No new material — the layout only

The label sits above the field and the hint below it, both in the flow with a reserved line, so turning a hint into an error does not move the field. An error that reflows the form is how you lose the field you were typing in. Severity is a rule and ink, never a colour — the same argument .swarn makes in settings, and the same one that keeps this legible in greyscale.

§12 · Both themes, four grounds

Same CSS, swapped tokens

The app follows prefers-color-scheme and has no manual toggle; these panels force the two token sets side by side. Nothing added in this proposal has a colour of its own — paper is still the instructive one, where --divot-src-hi collapses to the paper itself and every cut becomes shadow-only, which is what a groove in white paper actually looks like.

§14 · What taking this layer costs

  • Every button gets 2px smaller. The border is gone, so the box loses 1px a side. Either add it back as padding at the call sites, or accept a one-commit re-space of the toolbars. This is the whole migration; nothing else about the core's classes changes name or meaning.
  • Corners butt instead of mitering. A real border joins its four colours on the diagonal; offset bands join them square. At w:1 that is one pixel. At w:3 you can find it if you go looking.
  • forced-colors has to put a border back, because there is no longer one to override. Handled in §N9, but it is the kind of thing that goes missing in a refactor.
  • Two renderers for one wall. .pill draws a conic gradient and everything else draws bands. They agree at rest and they agree under the ladder, but they are two code paths — if the lighting model ever changes, it changes in two places. Only take the pill if you actually want pills.
  • Don't let width leak into buttons. The house rule is intact and is the easiest one to lose now that widening is one token away: a 22px icon toggle and a 44px primary get the same w:1, or the toolbar stops reading as a row of peers.
  • Radius is still a separate decision. This proves the primitive survives a radius; it does not argue you should have one. --r stays 0px and every rounded class here is opt-in.
